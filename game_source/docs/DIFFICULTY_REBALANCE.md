# Difficulty Rebalance

The live game already supplies three important anti-snowball systems: 36-week genre saturation, concurrent-release attention loss and prestige/franchise expectations. The expansion compounds these with competitive rights loss, imperfect appraisal, royalties, expensive rescue trade-offs, creator risk and long-lived capital commitments.

- Early: small/cult rights can be affordable, but cash tied in a bid cannot fund staff, production burn or marketing. Low-data decisions stay survivable rather than guaranteed.
- Mid: the same liquidity must cover office/facility growth, staff pressure, auctions, rescue and promotion. Rivals have enough genre/reputation information to contest attractive properties.
- Late: premium expectations amplify backlash; legendary rights and adaptation scope can create losses much larger than ordinary series. Capital projects provide capability objectives from £1.5m to £1.1bn.

Breakout quality can still overcome saturation because the market multiplier is not a hard ban. Rival and review noise remain bounded; deterministic economic rules are surfaced in breakdowns and rights cards.
